# Evidence-backed problem framing for the pitch

**Target user:** An older person with limited mobility who wants to retrieve a small everyday object without getting up or waiting for a carer. A carer can teach the task once, after which the system aims to help with repetitive retrieval tasks. It **supports**, rather than replaces, carers.

## Three grounded statistics

1. **Ageing:** WHO reports that by 2030 **1 in 6 people worldwide will be aged 60+**. Source: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/ageing-and-health (2025).
2. **Care workforce:** Skills for Care estimates around **96,000 vacant roles on a typical day in England's adult social care sector** in 2025/26, a vacancy rate of **6.2%**, the lowest in a decade but about three times the wider-economy rate. It projects **410,000 additional posts by 2040**. The reduction in vacancies is real; don't say it is currently rising. Source: https://www.skillsforcare.org.uk/news-and-events/news/adult-social-care-vacancy-rate-falls-to-lowest-level-in-a-decade-as-workforce-grows (24 June 2026). Related article: https://www.caretalk.co.uk/news/adult-social-care-vacancy-rate-falls-to-lowest-level-in-a-decade-as-workforce-grows/
3. **Longer lives:** McKinsey Health Institute / FII (2025) projects **1.6 billion people over 65 by 2050**, roughly double today's number, and notes the global support ratio falling towards **four working-age people per older adult**. Source: https://fii-institute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/THEECO2.pdf (executive summary, printed page 1, PDF page 4).

The 2024 McKinsey Health Institute / FII report is a useful *context* source about longevity and independence, not a demonstration that this robot will reduce care staffing needs: https://fii-institute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/45362619-4f7c-4cfa-9cb7-6878909d0d5f-1.pdf (executive summary, printed page 1, PDF page 3).

## 20-second problem statement

“As populations age, everyday retrieval tasks can become challenging for people with limited mobility. England still has roughly 96,000 adult social care vacancies, even though staffing has improved. We are prototyping an offline-first assistive robot that a carer can teach a simple household task, which it then plans and executes in MuJoCo while adapting to a moved object and verifying the outcome.”

## Honest innovation claim

**Not:** “We invented household-assistance robots” or “we trained a quadruped to learn any skill from one video.”

**Instead:** “We demonstrate a task-level teach-once assistant, grounded in new sensor observations, with offline request handling, explicit action validation and recovery.”

## Impact and limitations

A competition simulation does not establish real-world safety or care outcomes. Do not imply that robot use can replace carers or that falls have been prevented. If presenting impact measures, label them as target measures (task success %, successful recovery %, human interventions per run, elapsed time), not results until you actually collect them.
